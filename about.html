Our story

Why I’m doing this

Every reference site is written by somebody, for a reason. This page is where the reason goes: who built this, what it is trying to be, and what it deliberately is not.

The mission

To stop the software being the hard part of learning statistics — so that a beginner opening Stata for the first time spends their effort on the thinking, not on guessing what to type.

Free to use, written in plain English, and built for the week you feel furthest behind.

My story

How this site came about

I struggled with my Stata-19 coursework. Not briefly, and not in a way I could shrug off: the work took far longer than it should have, and for a while I could not tell whether I was finding it hard because the statistics was difficult or because I could not make the software do anything.

What I remember is how much of that time went on things that were not statistics at all — working out what to type, working out why it had not worked, and working out whether the problem was my understanding or my spelling. None of that was the part I was being assessed on, and all of it stood between me and the part I was.

What surprised me most was how little help there was. I went looking, more than once, and kept finding the same thing: plenty of Stata material, almost none of it written for somebody who had just opened the program for the first time.

I did not need more Stata resources. I needed one that assumed I was starting from nothing.

So I built the thing I had been looking for. A dictionary of commands you can search by what you are trying to do rather than by a name you have not met yet, so that typing “average” finds the command instead of returning nothing. A beginner guide that starts at opening Stata and ends with a finished analysis, written to build confidence rather than to prove anything. And a help centre for the moments when something breaks and you just need to know, quickly, what the message means.

I wanted it to look like a professional reference rather than a student project, because that is the kind of site I would have trusted when I was the one who was stuck. I built it with Claude Code, which meant I could spend my time deciding what should be in it instead of learning to build a website from scratch.

It started as a way to make my own life simpler. If it makes yours simpler too, that is the whole point of putting it online.

The problem

Beginner-friendly Stata help is surprisingly hard to find

There is no shortage of Stata material. There is a shortage of Stata material written for somebody who has never opened it. Almost everything available assumes you are already past the part that is actually stopping you.

  • The official documentation is precise, not welcoming. It is written as a reference for people who already know the vocabulary, so looking up summarize when you are not sure what a variable list is tends to raise more questions than it answers.
  • Forum answers assume the asker’s context. A reply that solves somebody else’s problem in one line is very hard to adapt when you cannot yet tell which part of it matters.
  • Textbooks teach the statistics, not the software. They will explain what a t-test is for and leave the typing as an exercise, which is exactly backwards if the typing is where you are stuck.
  • Videos move at the presenter’s pace. They are good for watching somebody work, and poor for the thing you usually need at eleven at night: one specific answer, findable in seconds.
  • Error messages explain nothing to a newcomer. r(111) and varlist required are perfectly clear once you know what they mean, and completely opaque until then.

None of that is anyone’s fault. Documentation is written by experts, forums are written for peers, and courses have a syllabus to get through. But the gap it leaves is real, and the people standing in it are beginners who conclude, wrongly, that they are the problem.

That was my own experience of looking: not that there was nothing out there, but that everything I found assumed I was further along than I was.

What this site aims to do

Four things, done properly

Not a course, not a textbook and not a replacement for either. Four narrow jobs, aimed at the gap described above.

Plain-English explanations

Every command is described by the problem it solves before the syntax appears, so you can find it by searching for what you want rather than for a word you have not met yet.

Open the dictionary

Confidence, not just answers

The guide teaches do-files from the first lesson, because knowing you can rerun everything is what makes mistakes stop being frightening. Nothing you type can break anything.

Start the guide

Quick answers when you are stuck

One search box over the errors, the questions and the glossary. Paste the code from an error message, read what it means in a sentence, and get back to the actual assignment.

Open the Help Centre

Honest about its limits

This site teaches the software. It does not tell you which analysis your question needs, and where it disagrees with your course materials, your course materials are right. It is also free, with no account and no tracking.

Where to start
